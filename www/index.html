From the editor

Why digitise a magazine?

GLOSS began as a stack of glossy paper on a newsstand: heavy covers, full-page portraits, and the faint smell of perfume samples. Issue 2.0 is not paper. It is a screen — scrollable, clickable, playable.

This site asks how digitisation changes the form, reach, and feel of fashion media. We kept the photography and the attitude, but swapped the paper weight for responsive layout, the perfume strip for a play button, and the monthly deadline for an endless feed.

— The editors, GLOSS 2.0 · From Glossy to Digital

Chapter 01

The Glossy Era

Before pixels, there was paper. A fashion magazine was a physical object: thick pages, a glossy cover, and a monthly ritual of visiting the newsstand. The layout was fixed, the photographs were large, and the reader turned one page at a time.

Chapter 02

From Pages to Screens

As magazines moved online, the page became a screen. Layouts learned to resize, images became galleries, and text gained links. The reader no longer flipped; they scrolled, tapped, and clicked through a story that could include sound and motion.

  • Responsive layout
  • Hyperlinks & navigation
  • Embedded images & video

# The Digital Reader

The digital reader is no longer a passive viewer. They like, save, comment, share, and send. Algorithms recommend new content, hashtags create communities, and links let a story travel across platforms in seconds.

  • Participate Comments, polls, and DMs turn reading into a conversation.
  • Share Reposts, Stories, and forwards make every reader a distributor.
  • Discover Hashtags, search, and recommendations help new readers find content.
  • Personalise Feeds adapt to taste, making every reader’s magazine slightly different.

Chapter 05

The Future of Fashion Media

Print is not dead, and digital is not everything. The future is a split screen: collectors still want the weight of paper, while daily readers want the speed of the feed. The magazines that survive will know which medium serves which story.
